# Object geometry record: BRIO World wooden railway track

## Identity and purpose

- Manufacturer: BRIO AB, now sold through Ravensburger's BRIO sites.
- Reference products: 33333 / article 63333300 Mini Straight Tracks (54 mm, EAN 7312350333336); 33334 / 63333400 Short Straight Tracks (108 mm, EAN 7312350333343); 33335 / 63333500 Medium Straight Tracks (144 mm, EAN 7312350333350); 33341 / 63334100 Long Straight Tracks (216 mm, EAN 7312350333411). [S1–S5]
- Family: conventional single-track wooden BRIO World railway with two wheel grooves and round-head/keyhole end joints. Plastic road, monorail, double-track, and specialty accessory interfaces fall outside this record.
- Hardware revision: unknown. Current product pages identify article/EAN, not machining revision, production date, or dimensional tolerance. Community measurements cover specimens of unspecified age; do not assume they describe a current factory drawing.
- Research date / author: 2026-10-03 / coding-assistant research from linked sources. No physical specimen measured in this workspace.
- Intended consumers: a short printable straight and a rolling wagon carrying DUPLO studs. This record covers the track and wheel guidance; the DUPLO clutch interface needs its own evidence.
- Readiness: **approximate with listed assumptions**, sufficient for printable trial-fit prototypes. Physical compatibility with the user's track, universal cross-brand compatibility, and child safety remain unverified.

**No manufacturer mechanical interface drawing or industry-certified dimensional standard was found.** Official sources establish product identity and advertised family lengths. Use community dimensions as evidence for a prototype, with their specimen and method limitations below.

## Appearance and assembly

A conventional straight looks like a pale wooden strip with two continuous parallel channels on a running face. The channels guide wheels; the raised strip between them and the strips along the outside remain wood, rather than separate metal rails or sleepers. BRIO smooths/bevels groove edges. [S2, S5, S7] The user identifies normal track as double-sided: the requested prototype therefore has matching channels on both faces by default, with a single-sided flat-underside option. This requirement does not establish every historical product's machining revision.

In plan, a male end has a narrow central neck and a round head. A female end has a matching keyhole-like through-cut: narrow entrance at the shoulder, wider circular pocket inside the body. The loose pocket permits some angular and longitudinal play. Lift/lower the head into the open socket to assemble; **do not force the head axially through the narrower throat** or infer a rigid-PLA snap fit. S2's official picture and S8's profile show an open full-height joint, not a concealed half-height cavity. [S2, S7, S8]

Curves resemble annular strips with the same paired channels following the arc and radial end shoulders carrying the same connector types. S7 describes standard curves as 45° segments, eight to a circle. Grooves on both faces of a reversible curve permit flipping it to the opposite handedness; S8 describes this feature, but it should not be assumed for every BRIO vintage/variant. Mini-track packs include connector-gender adjustment pieces, not just male/female straights. [S2, S7, S8]

## Units, datums and length convention

- All normalized dimensions are millimetres; angles use degrees.
- Default prototype origin: centre of the start/female shoulder on the underside, X=Y=Z=0. +X is its initial tangent; +Y crosses the track; +Z rises from the underside. Body sides sit at Y=±20 and top at Z=12 on level straights. Configured falls lift their high starting shoulder so the low endpoint remains Z=0.
- Adopted straight length `L` means **body shoulder to body shoulder, excluding male projection**. A male/female 54 mm body therefore has a 71.75 mm total X envelope with the adopted peg. Its assembled contribution with touching shoulders is 54 mm.
- Official pages advertise 54/108/144/216 mm but do not state an engineering datum. The shoulder interpretation is **inferred**, supported by S7's additive relations (2×54=108; 2×108=216; 3×144=2×216) and S8's explicit "without a plug" convention / body extrusion. It is not an official datum statement.
- `models/brio_duplo/track/geometry.py` uses this start-shoulder datum directly. For the default straight the body is X=0…54, socket centre +12, peg centre +66. The wagon inspection scene translates that default track by −27 mm; other curve/grade placements must use their configured endpoint frames.
- S8's variable `neck_length=12` measures **shoulder to circular-head/pocket centre**, not the exposed straight segment. Preserve that distinction when converting source dimensions.
- Diameters below mean complete circular size; radial allowances are per side. Gauge here means groove/tread **centre-to-centre**, not inner-edge spacing, wheel back-to-back, or axle length.

| Female throat | Nominal width 6.3, open from X=0 through pocket centre | **+0.20 per lateral side**, final width **6.7** | S8 width precedent with independent socket-only allowance. It stays narrower than 11.5 head; assemble vertically. |

The added 0.20 mm socket allowance is **not** the total mating clearance. Nominal 16 versus 11.5 already gives 2.25 mm radial play; the compensated 16.4 pocket gives **2.45 mm radial / 4.90 mm diametral play** for a coaxial head. The compensated throat gives 0.60 mm total width difference from a 6.1 neck. This prototype intentionally preserves loose wood-track-style positioning. Do not replace that slack with S8's 0.15 mm radial snug-print clearance without a different fit decision and coupon.

The free neck at its sidewall meets the adopted circular head about **7.126 mm** beyond the male shoulder: `12 − sqrt(5.75² − 3.05²)`. The nominal socket's sidewall throat meets its circle about **4.646 mm** inward: `12 − sqrt(8² − 3.15²)`. These derivations explain the approximate 7 / 5 mm prose measurements without treating them as centre distances. The source does not prove that this was S7's measuring convention; that reconciliation remains an inference.

Printing assumptions: FDM, material/nozzle/shrinkage/calibration unknown. The double-sided default has downward-facing 6 mm channel roofs; bridge quality/support strategy is unresolved and must be evaluated in the slicer. The single-sided option has a flat bed-facing underside. Remove first-layer spread from actual mating surfaces or calibrate slicer compensation. Vertical entry into a full-height socket avoids a socket roof. No strength or safety claim follows from export success.

### Configurable basic-track family

One `models/brio_duplo/track/model.py` consumes a TOML `TrackConfig`; preset files
select straight length, independent start/end genders, signed horizontal turn
angle and centreline radius, endpoint rise/fall, and either groove-face option.
Default is a 54 mm female/male double-sided straight. Male/male and female/female
are the same model, not special duplicated geometries. Splitters/mergers are
outside this basic single-route model and should have separate entrypoints.

Radius 202 and 110 mm presets derive from S7's approximate large/small body
centre radii, not the undefined official 170/90 mm marketed labels. Planar curves
use exact arcs; a curve's horizontal shoulder-route length is `R * abs(angle)`
in radians. It is not its chord, nominal straight `length`, or overall envelope.
Graded pieces use original 24 mm level end pads and a cubic eased elevation
transition, with explicitly oriented smooth-loft sections at ≤3 mm horizontal
spacing. Groove dimensions are normal to the local section on the transition.
The ±0.04 mm ideal-route material sampling check is CAD acceptance, not factory
tolerance or a guaranteed global surface-error bound. These rise/fall profiles
are design choices, not researched BRIO bridge replacement specifications.


## Wagon guidance, not a replacement-wheel specification

A wagon wheel can roll **inside** each groove, using the groove walls for lateral guidance. A flat 4 mm tread at centres Y=±13 leaves `(6−4)/2 = 1.0 mm` per side in an ideal rectangular groove. This is a useful straight-track prototype, distinct from claiming that BRIO uses that exact wheel profile or a particular flange geometry.

| Wagon parameter | Trial value / derivation | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| Rolling/tread centres | 26 apart, Y=±13 | Derived from adopted track gauge; not wheel back-to-back. |
| Tread width | 4 | Assumed design choice; 1 mm nominal side gap in 6 mm channels. On S8's 4.75 mm floor with 25.7 gauge, minimum centred side gap falls to `0.375−0.15 = 0.225` before printing errors. |
| Wheel diameter | 16 | Assumed design choice; selected to leave collar/track clearance, not a sourced BRIO wheel size. |
| Wheel vertical placement on this track | Groove floor Z=9; axle Z=9+8=17; wheel top Z=25 | Derived for a 16 mm unflanged tread riding the floor. A different flange/running surface changes this. |
| Narrow bearing/chassis keel | 18 wide, 12 tall, edges Y=±9; underside Z=14 in assembly | Assumed design: inner hub face at Y=±10 gives 1 mm hub gap; underside clears Z=12 lands by 2 mm. |
| Wagon deck envelope | 63.8 × 31.8 × 4; underside Z=26, seating plane Z=30 | Design derived from DUPLO grid record; 1 mm above wheel top. Not a BRIO dimension. |
| Axle shaft, bearings and retention | No adopted factory requirement | S9 supplies only a 2 × 32 mm metal-shaft precedent. Select bearings / captured axle retention from the wagon design and physical tests, not that precedent alone. |

No traceable official wheel tread diameter, flange width/depth, back-to-back, axle-hole tolerance, production wheel gauge, or acceptable wagon wheelbase was found. S9 is enough to identify a modern axle precedent but not reproduce a complete wheelset. Derive a simple printed wheel from the intended groove and label it assumed. Straight running does not prove curve running: a rigid wheelbase can bind against groove sidewalls on E/E1 curves, and joint slack can create a derailment step. Require a curve specimen test before claiming curved-track compatibility.

### Verification plan and results

Integration built `track`, `wagon`, `wheel`, and `assembly` through the real STL/3MF/STEP exporter: each printable component has one valid solid; the assembly has 12. Native/STEP checks and watertight mesh/3MF round trips passed. The configurable smoke exported 12 variants under `outputs/brio_duplo/variants/`, recording each exact configuration and native/STEP connector mating in its report. All six straight end/face combinations, left/right/tighter curves, rise, fall, and a rising curve passed. The flipped default and two joined default tracks cleared the wagon at five sampled positions. PNG iso/top/bottom/front views were inspected for all variants and components; earlier straight/assembly HTML views were inspected interactively. Screenshots of the graded-curve HTML timed out; cause and outstanding interactive inspection are tracked in [#25](https://github.com/ShakataGaNai/b123d/issues/25). These results establish sampled CAD geometry, not physical running, printing or fit.
